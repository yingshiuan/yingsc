#### Bridging emerging technologies and human experience

New technology usually arrives as a capability, not an experience. A language model returns a wall of text. A motion tracker returns coordinates. A robotic arm returns a toolpath.<span class="material-symbols-outlined">precision_manufacturing</span> My work is the layer in between — turning what a technology *can* do into something a person can actually use.

That is the thread through everything I have built, and it is why I care less about whether a pipeline is clever than about whether someone understands what comes out of it.

Done well, that layer feels a little like magic<span class="material-symbols-outlined">auto_awesome</span>— not because it hides the machinery, but because it meets people where they already are. A wall of retrieved text becomes a map you can follow. A tracked pen becomes a brush. The technology does not get simpler; the distance between it and the person does.

#### How I got here

I trained as an architect in Taiwan, designing spaces that connect people. But I kept drifting toward the digital side: computational design, digital and robotic fabrication, programming robotic arms to build physical structures. That pull took me to ETH Zürich for a [Master's in Architecture and Digital Fabrication](https://masdfab.arch.ethz.ch/), where I found I liked building the tools as much as the buildings.

Architecture left me two habits I still use every day — designing for real constraints, and thinking about the person who has to live with the result.

#### Where that has taken me

At [DisneyResearch|Studios](https://studios.disneyresearch.com)<span class="material-symbols-outlined">computer</span> — a lab where storytelling and engineering sit in the same room — I built AI-powered data platforms and interactive web applications, including the visualization layer that made retrieval results explorable rather than merely accurate.

At [Sensoryx](https://sensoryx.tech)<span class="material-symbols-outlined">view_in_ar</span>, I built XR prototypes in Unity around a motion-tracked stylus with exactly one physical button — painting, grabbing, and selecting all had to fit through it. I refined those gesture workflows for both B2B and consumer users, inside that hardware constraint.

Before and since, I have shipped production frontends, rebuilt an e-commerce experience that lifted orders by 35%<span class="material-symbols-outlined">web</span>, and built a full-stack menu platform for a Zürich restaurant.
